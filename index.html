Worked examples

Gravel for a driveway — 24 × 11 ft, 3.5 in deep

Area: 24 × 11 = 264 sq ft. Volume: 264 × (3.5 ÷ 12) = 77 cu ft. That is 77 ÷ 27 = 2.85 yd³ before waste. Add 10% waste for compaction and uneven ground: 2.85 × 1.1 = 3.14 yd³ (84.7 cu ft), about 4.71 tons.

Bags: 84.7 cu ft ÷ 0.5 cu ft per bag = 169.4, so 170 bags. Check it yourself with the free gravel calculator.

Mulch for a bed — 200 sq ft, 3 in deep

Volume: 200 × (3 ÷ 12) = 50 cu ft = 50 ÷ 27 = 1.85 yd³. That is about 2 cubic yards — order 2, or 37 bags at 1.35 cu ft each. Try the free mulch calculator.

Concrete for a slab — 10 × 10 ft, 4 in thick

Volume: 100 × (4 ÷ 12) = 33.3 cu ft = 1.23 yd³. With 10% waste: 1.23 × 1.1 = 1.36 yd³. A 10 × 10 ft slab at 4 inches needs about 1.4 cubic yards — order 1.5 to be safe, or use the free concrete calculator to get the bag count for your mix.

Sod for a lawn — 50 × 40 ft

Area: 50 × 40 = 2,000 sq ft. With 5% waste for cutting and fitting: 2,000 × 1.05 = 2,100 sq ft. At 450 sq ft per pallet that is 2,100 ÷ 450 = 4.67, so 5 pallets (about 210 rolls). Work it out with the free sod calculator.

Fence posts — 6-ft posts, 30 in frost depth, 12-in holes, 14 posts

Hole depth: 36 in (a third of the post plus frost allowance). Concrete per hole: about 4 bags of 50-lb mix. Total: 14 × 4 = 56 bags. Confirm your numbers with the free fence post depth calculator.

Paver base for a driveway — 11 × 19 ft

Compacted base at 4 in: 4.26 yd³, about 6.81 tons, or 230 bags. Use the free paver base calculator to adjust depth and waste for your own driveway.
